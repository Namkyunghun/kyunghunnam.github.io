MATRIX-AWARE OPTIMIZATION

Theory and Design of Matrix-Aware Optimizers

I analyze matrix-structured optimizers—including Shampoo, SOAP, and Muon—to understand their geometry, convergence, stability, and numerical error. I use these insights to design faster and more reliable optimization methods for large-scale deep learning.

RESEARCH THESIS

Matrix structure is useful only when its optimization gains survive computation.

My research asks a central question: How can matrix structure in neural-network parameters and gradients be converted into optimization gains that survive approximation, finite precision, and large-scale implementation?

01

Understand

Characterize the geometry, convergence, stability, and spectral behavior induced by matrix-valued update maps.

02

Quantify

Measure how staleness, damping, eigenspace drift, matrix-function approximation, and finite precision alter the intended operator.

03

Design

Turn theoretical and numerical insights into adaptive, efficient, and reliable optimizer mechanisms.

Kyunghun Nam and Sumyeong Ahn

CURRENT QUESTIONS

Problems that connect operator theory to optimizer design.

01

Induced geometry

What geometry is actually induced by Shampoo, SOAP, and Muon, and which notion of steepest descent does each approximate?

02

Approximation fidelity

Which optimizer properties survive approximate inverse roots, eigenspace reuse, and polynomial matrix iterations?

03

Numerical dynamics

How do staleness, damping, scaling, and finite precision alter convergence and training stability?

04

Observable control

Can inexpensive operator-error signals decide when to refresh, regularize, or change the update map?

05

Method selection

When should training use preconditioning, spectral normalization, or a hybrid matrix-aware update?

ABOUT

Optimization theory, matrix analysis, and numerical algorithms.

I am a Ph.D. student in Artificial Intelligence at the Korea Institute of Energy Technology (KENTECH), advised by Prof. Sumyeong Ahn. My research lies at the intersection of optimization theory, matrix analysis, numerical linear algebra, and large-scale deep learning.

I am particularly interested in identifying which aspects of matrix-aware optimizers are structural, which are artifacts of approximation or implementation, and how that distinction can guide new methods.
